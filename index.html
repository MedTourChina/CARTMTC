What Is CAR-T Therapy?

CAR-T (Chimeric Antigen Receptor T-cell) therapy is a form of immunotherapy that reprograms a patient's own T cells to recognize and attack cancer cells. It is sometimes called a "living drug" because the engineered cells can persist and continue protecting the body for years.

🧬

Your T cells → genetically engineered → expanded → infused back → they hunt cancer.

A Personalized, One-Time Infusion

Unlike conventional chemotherapy or radiation, CAR-T is built for one specific patient. A sample of the patient's blood is collected, the T cells are modified in the laboratory to carry a synthetic receptor (the "CAR") aimed at a marker on the cancer, multiplied into the millions, and returned to the patient as a single infusion.

In blood cancers such as certain leukemias, lymphomas and multiple myeloma, CAR-T has produced remissions where almost every other treatment had failed.

How CAR-T Works — In Five Steps

From a simple blood draw to a life-changing infusion, the CAR-T process is carefully staged and monitored by specialized hematology teams.

1

Collect (Apheresis)

White blood cells are collected from the patient's blood through a few-hours apheresis procedure.

2

Engineer & Expand

In the lab, T cells are given the CAR gene and grown into millions of cancer-seeking cells.

3

Lymphodepletion

A short course of conditioning chemotherapy prepares the body to receive the new cells.

4

Infusion

The engineered CAR-T cells are infused back — a process similar to a blood transfusion.

5

Monitoring

Patients are closely watched for responses and manageable side effects like CRS.

🎉

Response

Many patients achieve deep, durable remissions — the goal of every CAR-T program.

Which Cancers Can CAR-T Treat?

CAR-T was born in blood cancers and is now expanding into solid tumors through active clinical research. Explore the indications by target and disease.

🨄

Leukemia

CD19 CAR-T for relapsed/refractory B-cell acute lymphoblastic leukemia (ALL), including pediatric and adult patients.

🩷

Lymphoma

The most established CAR-T field — diffuse large B-cell, follicular, mantle cell and other relapsed/refractory lymphomas.

🩸

Multiple Myeloma

BCMA-targeted CAR-T has reshaped treatment for patients whose myeloma relapsed after multiple lines of therapy.

🔮

Solid Tumors

An active research frontier (GPC3, Claudin18.2, etc.). Not yet broadly approved — available mainly through clinical trials.
