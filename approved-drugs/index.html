Why Does One Infusion Cost More Than ¥1 Million?

CAR-T is not a chemical that can be manufactured by the tonne. Every dose is a batch of one, made from one patient's cells, and released only after that patient is already waiting.

🧬

One patient, one batch

Cells are collected by apheresis, genetically engineered, expanded for roughly two weeks, tested, frozen and shipped back. Nothing is shared between patients, so every step carries a full fixed cost for a single dose.

🦠

Viral vector and consumables

The gene-delivery vector alone has historically cost US$15,000–30,000 per patient. A single set of imported cell-processing consumables has been reported at over ¥100,000. Both line items sit inside the price of the drug.

🏭

Capacity, not demand

Traditional manual cleanroom production supports only tens of patients per site per year. Fixed costs are spread across very few doses, and a failed batch means a patient is treated weeks later — or not at all.

🛡

Safety is part of the product

Every dose requires release testing, chain-of-identity verification, cytogenetic monitoring and a hospital able to manage cytokine release syndrome and neurological toxicity with ICU backup. That infrastructure is not free.

Context worth knowing: since the first Chinese approval in 2021, the nine approved products together have treated only a few thousand patients. The bottleneck has not been eligibility — it has been price.

How Patients Actually Pay

No CAR-T product is covered by China's national basic medical insurance. Payment instead comes from a stack of city-level and commercial schemes built up over the past few years.

💰

1. Self-pay baseline

The published list price is what an uninsured patient is charged for the product. Everything else in the table above starts from this number.

🏦

2. City supplementary schemes

More than 60% of China's city-level "Huiminbao" schemes now include CAR-T as a special drug benefit. Reimbursement is typically 60–90% for healthy enrolees and 20–40% for those with pre-existing conditions.

📋

3. Commercial drug catalogue

China's first Commercial Health Insurance Innovative Drug Catalogue took effect on 1 January 2026 and includes five CAR-T products. Private medical policies covering CAR-T can reimburse up to 100% of the drug cost.

🤝

4. Manufacturer programmes

Several makers run patient assistance and instalment arrangements through hospitals, and competition is pushing list prices down directly — see the trend below.

Important for international patients: the city and national schemes above require Chinese residency and local enrolment, so they are generally not available to patients treated as international visitors. International patients normally pay the published product price plus hospital service costs, and our team confirms an itemised quotation before you travel. Please also note that city schemes are tightening: Shanghai's 2026 Huihubao cut reimbursement for enrolees with pre-existing conditions to 30% and added a per-drug annual cap.

The Price Is Falling — Three Forces at Work

The ¥1.2 million price of 2021 is no longer the floor of the market. Each of these developments pushes the entry point lower.

📉

Domestic competition

Four CD19 products now compete for the same lymphoma patients. The newest entrant, 恒凯莱, launched at about ¥899,000 — roughly 25% below the first product to market — and entered CSCO lymphoma guidance in 2026.

⚙

Full-chain localisation

A Shanghai developer has filed for approval of a CAR-T priced at ¥230,000–250,000, built on home-made equipment, consumables and an automated closed production line rather than imported manual workflow. Its filing was accepted in April 2026.

🔬

Off-the-shelf and in-vivo

Allogeneic products made from healthy donors could be manufactured in batches, and in-vivo CAR-T would skip the laboratory entirely by engineering T cells inside the body. Both are in clinical development in China.

What this means for you: if your disease is not immediately life-threatening, it is worth asking your specialist whether a currently approved product, a newer lower-priced entrant, or a clinical trial is the better route. If it is urgent, the decision should be made on eligibility and hospital capability, not on waiting for a price that may not arrive in time.

Beyond Blood Cancer: The First Solid-Tumour CAR-T

In June 2026 the NMPA approved 恺力美 (satricabtagene autoleucel), the first CAR-T anywhere in the world approved for a solid tumour. It targets CLDN18.2 in advanced gastric and gastro-oesophageal junction adenocarcinoma that is HER2-negative and has progressed after at least two lines of therapy.

It is priced at ¥990,000. Media reports describe a patient from New Zealand with advanced gastro-duodenal adenocarcinoma travelling to Shanghai for treatment after chemotherapy and immunotherapy failed — among the first international patients treated with the product.

How to Read This List

🎯

Target = the aim

CD19 CAR-Ts treat B-cell cancers (leukaemias, lymphomas). BCMA CAR-Ts treat multiple myeloma. CLDN18.2 CAR-Ts treat gastric and junctional adenocarcinoma. The target determines which disease a product can address.

📅

Indication = the approval

A product may be approved for one or more specific, often relapsed/refractory, conditions. Exact eligible lines of therapy differ by country and product — and several products carry more than one indication.

🏢

Availability = the reality

Which product and indication are actually accessible to an international patient depends on the treating hospital's accreditation and stock. We match you to a centre that holds the product you need.

Important: Drug names, approvals, indications and prices change. This page is educational and reflects publicly reported information as of October 2026. Your specialist will confirm the precise product, indication and eligibility for your case.
